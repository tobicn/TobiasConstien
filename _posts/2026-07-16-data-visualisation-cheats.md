I like data visualisations. Presenting complex data and analytical models within an aesthetically-pleasing figure or plot can do a lot in regars to research communication and making research more approachable and inclusive. Havin said that, let's not cheat with data visualisations.

Who's got the bigger one?
---

I came across a recent example of data visualisations cheats at a recent visit to the Stuttgart TV tower in Germany. The TV tower in Stuttgart is, in fact, the first in the world of its kind, and architecturally it really is stunning. Do yourself a favor and visit if you are in the area. There's a lot to be proud of in this landmark. Its size (i.e., 217m), however, not so much - though you can try!

The graphic that is presented to visitors after entry in front of the elevator shows graphical depictions of TV towers across the world seemingly _at scale_. We see a TV tower from Munich (260m), Kairo (217m), or Tokyo (634m). Graphically, we are led to believe that the Stuttgart TV tower, after all the first of its kind, outranks them all in size. But look just a little bit closer, and you'll notice that something is up. Stuttgart, with its 217m, is (as it turns out with a bit of math), only a bit over a third of the size of Tokyo's Sky Tree. Sure doesn't seem that way from the graph!

![TV Towers across the world at (corrected) scale](https://github.com/tobicn/TobiasConstien/blob/master/images/StuttgartTV-Tower-Scaled.png?raw=true)

I took the time to redraw this graph by size. This is what it should actually look like. @SWR, if you want to update your graph (and stop lying with data visualisations), you can find it in my GitHub.

I also don't want to lie with data visualistions, so I should acknowledge here that I had the [image](https://github.com/tobicn/TobiasConstien/blob/master/images/StuttgartTV-Tower-Picture.jpg?raw=true) that I took of the graph during my visit redrawn by ChatGPT to correct glare and contrast. I checked that the presented sizes remained unchanged.

That’s all.
