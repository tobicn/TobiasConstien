**Background**: As digital devices have become increasingly mobile, estimates of toddlers' screen time have surged far beyond public health recommendations. Yet, the immediate and long-term impact of screen time on toddlers’ development is not well understood.

**Aims and Objectives**: This mixed-methods study aimed to explore toddlers’ screen time and its associations with their hot and cool executive functioning. 
Research Design: Parents of toddlers (18-36 months, N = 192) from across Ireland responded to an online survey, including quantitative measures of toddlers’ screen time and cognitive development, as well as open-ended questions exploring parents’ perceptions of the nature of toddlers’ screen time. 

**Results**: Screen time showed negative associations with toddlers’ executive function development, meaning toddlers who had longer amounts of screen time were also rated lower in executive functioning by their parents. Importantly, the effect on hot executive functions was moderated by toddlers’ quality of sleep, indicating that sleep may play a protective role in toddlers’ cognitive development. Parental reports indicated that these aversive effects of screen time may not be immediately obvious, potentially leading parents to underestimate the negative effects of screen time on their toddlers’ development.
