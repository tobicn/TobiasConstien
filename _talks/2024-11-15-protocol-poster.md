# Abstract

**Proposition**: This prospective project seeks to examine associations between pretend play and executive functions in toddlerhood across three unique studies. First, it aims to provide a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies on the association of pretend play and executive functions throughout early development. Secondly, it will conduct an empirical, longitudinal examination of children’s pretend play and executive functions in the toddlerhood period. Lastly, it will develop a play-based measurement tool of executive functions to contribute to a refined understanding of executive functions and their correlates in toddlerhood.
**Discussion**: Play is a universal activity for children and has consistently been associated with developmental success. However, there is a lack of research exploring associations between play and executive functions. This lack of research is surprising, given the importance of executive functions in children’s development, and the congruence between processes of play and components of executive functions. Particularly pretend play, which engages children’s imagination and creativity, is thought to be beneficial for executive functions. 
**Implications**: Findings from this research are expected to inform theoretical claims of the developmental contributions of play, enhance the assessment of toddler’s executive functions and add to the empirical base for interventions aimed at promoting early learning skills.
